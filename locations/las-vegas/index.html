Older homes, newer homes

Las Vegas concrete problems usually start underground

The ground under the valley is hard on concrete. Knowing what's down there is half the job.

Much of Las Vegas sits on caliche, a cement-hard layer that can turn a simple dig into a slow one. Other lots have expansive soils that swell when sprinklers or a rare storm soak them, then shrink back as they dry. Slabs poured without good base prep and control joints crack along the way.

In established neighborhoods like Huntridge and the streets around Rancho, decades of that movement, plus sun-bleached color and old patches that never matched, leave a lot of concrete looking worse than it really is. In newer areas like Centennial Hills and Skye Canyon, the issue is usually the opposite: a plain builder slab that's too small for how you actually live.

Both have good answers. Sound concrete can often be repaired and resurfaced with an overlay. A new pour can be stamped or textured to look like stone or wood.

Fix it or replace it?

  • Usually fixableHairline cracks, faded or blotchy color, stains, flaking, or rough spots on a slab that hasn't moved.
  • Usually replaceSections that have sunk or lifted, wide cracks with one side higher, or crumbling concrete.

Why does concrete crack so much in Las Vegas?

Mostly the ground and the heat. Caliche and expansive soils move under slabs, especially when irrigation keeps them wet, and concrete that dries too fast in summer is weaker at the surface. Good base prep, control joints, and proper curing prevent most of it.

Do I need a permit to widen my driveway in Las Vegas?

Often, yes. Widening a driveway or changing the curb cut usually needs a permit from the city, or from Clark County if you're in an unincorporated area. We'll go over what applies to your address at the estimate.

Can you match new concrete to my old concrete?

Gray concrete rarely matches exactly, because old concrete has weathered. When a perfect match matters, a stain or overlay over both old and new gives you one even color.

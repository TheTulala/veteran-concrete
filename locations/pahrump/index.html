Bigger lots, heavier loads

Pahrump concrete has to carry more weight

A slab that only sees a car is one thing. A pad under a loaded fifth-wheel or a shop floor under a lift is another.

About an hour west of Las Vegas over the Spring Mountains, Pahrump is in Nye County, and half-acre to five-acre lots are the norm. Many properties have their own well and septic system, and many have no HOA, so you have more freedom in what you build. That's good news, but it also puts more weight, literally, on getting the base right.

Heavy vehicles need thicker concrete, compacted base, and the right reinforcement. Ground that has never been graded can hide soft spots or a hard caliche layer. And septic tanks and leach fields have to stay clear of driveways and pads, so the layout matters as much as the pour.

Before we pour on your lot

  • Know your septicMark the tank and leach field so driveways and pads stay clear of them.
  • Plan for the heaviest loadTell us what will park there, from a car to a loaded RV.
  • Check the permitShop slabs and outbuildings usually go through Nye County Building and Safety.

Do you come out to Pahrump for estimates?

Yes. Pahrump is one of our service areas, and estimates are free. Call or send the form with your address and we'll set a time to walk the property with you.

How thick should an RV pad be?

Thicker than a standard driveway, with compacted base and reinforcement sized for the load. The right answer depends on the RV's weight and your soil, so we spec it at the estimate rather than guessing.

Do I need a permit for a shop slab in Pahrump?

A slab for a garage, shop, or other building usually goes through Nye County Building and Safety along with the structure. Replacing an existing driveway in the same footprint generally doesn't. We'll help you sort out what applies.

More yard, less gravel

The builder slab was never meant to be the whole patio

Newer homes tend to come with a slab just big enough for a grill and two chairs, plus side yards full of rock that's hard to walk on and harder to keep clean.

Spring Valley, west of the Strip, and Enterprise, to the south around Mountain's Edge and Southern Highlands, are unincorporated communities. That means permits run through Clark County rather than a city, and driveway aprons have to be built to the county's standard drawings.

Inside your own lot, the options open up. Extend the patio and stamp it so the old and new sections read as one. Pour a side yard so bins and bikes roll easily. Add a pad for a hot tub or shed. Many of these communities also have HOAs, so check your guidelines before work starts.

Who issues permits in Spring Valley and Enterprise?

Both are unincorporated, so permits come from Clark County, not the City of Las Vegas. A ground-level patio often doesn't need one, but driveway widening and new curb cuts usually do. We'll go over what applies at the estimate.

Can you pour concrete in my side yard?

Yes. Side yards are a common upgrade. We slope the concrete so water drains away from the house and leave room for any utility access you need.

Is it better to extend my patio or replace it?

If the existing slab is sound, extending it and finishing both with a stamp, stain, or overlay is usually less work and less money than tearing it out. If it's cracked and moving, replacing it is the better choice.

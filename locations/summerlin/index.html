Design review first

Summerlin concrete starts with an approval

In Summerlin, almost any change you can see from outside needs written approval before the first form goes in.

Summerlin is a master-planned community with two layers of rules: community-wide design guidelines, and the guidelines of your own village association. Widening a driveway, switching from plain concrete to stamped or pavers, adding a side apron, and visible hardscape all go through design review. Pool projects usually need deck material and color samples in the package.

Review can take a few weeks, so it pays to start early and submit once. A vague quote leads to questions and resubmittals. A bid that clearly lays out the size, location, pattern, and color gives the committee what it needs the first time.

Most Summerlin homes lean toward desert earth tones and natural stone looks, which is exactly where stamped concrete shines. Patterns like flagstone and slate fit right in.

Planning a Summerlin project

  • Know your villageYour village association's guidelines sit on top of the community-wide rules.
  • Ask for samplesColor and pattern samples help both you and the committee decide.
  • Build in timeLeave room for review before a pool build, a party, or a listing date.

What needs design review in Summerlin?

Nearly any exterior change visible from outside, including driveway widening, a change from plain concrete to stamped, side aprons, and front-yard hardscape. Pool decks usually need material and color details. Check with your village association and get written approval before work begins.

Can I switch my driveway to stamped concrete?

Usually, with approval. Stamped concrete in a natural stone pattern and an earth-tone color is a common upgrade. Your bid will list the pattern and color so you can include them with your request.

How long does design review take?

It varies by village and time of year, but plan on a few weeks. A complete submission the first time is the best way to avoid delays.

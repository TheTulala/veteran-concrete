Older town, older concrete

Boulder City concrete deserves repair before replacement

Boulder City was built in the 1930s to house the workers who built Hoover Dam, and some of its walks and steps have been around almost as long.

The town's historic district is on the National Register of Historic Places, and a long-standing growth-control ordinance keeps new construction limited. The result is a town full of homes people hold on to and improve, rather than tear down and replace.

That makes repair and resurfacing a natural fit. A sound but tired slab can be repaired, overlaid, and recolored to look new, without the demolition, dust, and dump fees. When a section has truly failed, we'll tell you plainly and replace only what needs replacing.

If your home is in the historic district, check with the city before changing anything visible from the street.

Is your concrete worth saving?

  • Often yesSurface cracks, faded or stained color, rough or flaking spots on a slab that hasn't moved.
  • Often noHeaved or sunken sections, wide offset cracks, or concrete that crumbles when you scrape it.
  • Not sure?We'll look at it for free and give you a straight answer.

Can old concrete really be made to look new?

If it's structurally sound, yes. Cracks are repaired, the surface is prepped, and an overlay or stain gives it a fresh color and texture. If the slab is moving or crumbling, replacement is the honest answer.

Do I need a permit for a new driveway apron?

Yes. Work in the public right-of-way, like a driveway apron, needs a right-of-way permit from Boulder City and inspections during construction. Work entirely on your own lot follows different rules, and we'll go over them at the estimate.

How long does resurfacing take?

Most patio and walkway resurfacing jobs take a few working days, plus time for the sealer to cure before heavy use. You'll get a timeline with your bid.

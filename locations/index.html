Built for the desert

Concrete that holds up in Southern Nevada

Desert concrete has its own set of enemies, and they're the same in every corner of the valley.

Parts of the valley sit on caliche, a cement-hard layer that makes digging unpredictable. Other spots have expansive soils that swell when they get wet and shrink as they dry, pushing on whatever sits on top. Summer heat pulls water out of fresh concrete fast, so rushed finishing and skipped curing show up later as flaking and wandering cracks.

None of that is a reason to avoid concrete. It's a reason to hire a crew that digs, compacts, reinforces, and cures with the ground and the climate in mind, and that seals decorative work with a UV-stable sealer so the color lasts.

Working in your neighborhood

Is your license good outside Las Vegas?

Yes. Veteran Concrete's contractor license, #0094181 (C-08 Concrete), is issued by the Nevada State Contractors Board and covers work across the state. You can verify it with the board at (702) 486‑1100.

Do I need a permit for a patio or driveway?

It depends on where you live and what you're building. A ground-level patio often doesn't need a building permit, while widening a driveway or changing a curb cut usually means a permit from the city or county. We'll go over what applies to your project at the estimate.

My HOA has to approve the work. What do I need?

Most review committees want to see the size, location, pattern, and color of the new concrete. Your bid spells those out, so you can include it with your request. Get approval before work starts.

I don't see my area. Will you still come out?

Ask us. Call (805) 717‑5173 or send a few details with your location, and we'll give you a straight answer about whether we can take the job.

Beyond plain gray

Plain concrete shows everything and grips nothing

A smooth gray slab is the cheapest finish there is, and it looks like it. Every tire mark, oil drip, rust ring, and hairline crack stands out, and the glare off it in July is brutal.

Smooth concrete also gets slick when it's wet, which matters on a sloped driveway after a desert downpour or on a pool deck every single day. A textured, colored finish fixes both problems at once: it hides wear, and it gives your feet and tires something to grab.

Texture is also the most affordable way into decorative concrete. You get real character and a custom look on a patio, walkway, or drive without paying for a full stamped pattern, and our crew finishes it by hand with the owner on site.

Choosing a concrete texture

  • GripPool decks, steps, and sloped drives need more tooth than a covered patio.
  • HeatLighter colors and open textures stay cooler in the Las Vegas sun.
  • UpkeepDeeper textures hide dirt and wear. All decorative finishes last longer sealed.
  • StyleMatch the stucco, roof tile, and desert landscaping instead of fighting them.

Finishes we install

Concrete textures and colored concrete finishes

From practical to showpiece. Mix them, too: a broom-finished drive with a textured stone border is a sharp combination.

  • Wood grain

    Plank lines, grain, and knots pressed into the surface for the warmth of wood that won't rot, warp, or splinter.

  • Stone and slate texture

    Seamless stone texture with no grout lines. Natural looking, comfortable to walk on, and great on patios.

  • Broom finish

    The workhorse. Fine or heavy broom lines for traction on driveways, sidewalks, and sloped walks.

  • Rock salt finish

    Small pitted texture that feels great barefoot and grips when wet. A popular pool deck choice.

  • Exposed aggregate

    The top layer is washed back to reveal the rock in the mix. Rugged, grippy, and right at home with desert landscaping.

  • Colored concrete

    Integral color mixed through the whole slab, in earth tones that suit Las Vegas homes. It never wears off like paint.

What's the difference between textured and stamped concrete?

Stamped concrete presses a repeating pattern like brick or flagstone into the slab, with visible joints between the shapes. Textured concrete adds surface feel and grain without the defined pattern, using seamless texture skins, brooms, rock salt, or exposed aggregate. Texturing often costs less and gives better grip.

Which concrete texture is best around a pool?

Rock salt, light broom, or a knockdown finish in a light color. They grip well with wet feet and stay cooler in the Las Vegas sun than dark or smooth concrete.

Does colored concrete fade in the sun?

Integral color is mixed all the way through the concrete, so it never wears off like paint. Strong sun can soften the surface tone over the years, and a UV-stable sealer slows that down.

Can you add texture to existing concrete?

Yes. A concrete overlay puts a new surface on top of a sound existing slab, and that new surface can be textured, stamped, or colored. It's a common way to upgrade a plain gray patio or walk without tearing it out.
